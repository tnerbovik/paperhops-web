Why paper

Real paper, real crayons, real time together.

Screens aren’t the enemy, but most families would like a little less of them. A page to colour is an easy swap: quick to start, nothing to charge, and right there on the kitchen table.

  • ◐

    Calm focus

    Colouring is slow on purpose. One page can hold a child’s attention, with no notifications and no next video starting by itself.

  • ✎

    Busy hands

    Holding a crayon, staying near the lines and joining the dots are good practice for small hands.

  • ✦

    Their own ideas

    No right colours and no score. A purple lion is a perfectly good lion.

  • ✿

    Time together

    Sit down beside them. Colouring works across generations, from three to ninety-three.

Three levels

One lion. Three ways to colour it.

Each of the 300 subjects is drawn three times, from big friendly shapes to fine detail. Siblings, cousins and grandparents can colour the same picture, each at their own level.

  • 3–5

    Little artists

    Big shapes and thick lines for small hands and chunky crayons.

  • 6–8

    Young explorers

    Picture-book drawings with more to discover and colour in.

  • 9+

    Creative masters

    Realistic, detailed pages for older kids, and relaxing adult colouring for parents and grandparents.

Dot-to-dot

Join the dots. Then colour it in.

Every simple drawing also comes as a dot-to-dot. Counting from one dot to the next turns into a stegosaurus, a cat or a rocket, and then into a colouring page.

  • A dot-to-dot for each of the 300 subjects
  • Counting practice that ends in a picture
  • Prints just like a colouring page

For grandparents

Print a stack before they visit.

Paperhops is easy to use without fuss: pick a subject, choose an age and press Print. There is nothing to set up and no account to create.
